In Japan, your My Number health insurance card can connect your care across institutions. But a useful medical fact is still often trapped inside a record. What if you could prove the fact, without handing over the record?

This is MynaHealth. Think of it as a private proof layer for Japanese healthcare. A clinic, insurer, or online consultation service can state a narrow policy: for example, has this person received an eligible treatment within the required window? The patient can answer yes with a cryptographic proof. Their name, exact medication, date, and source document remain hidden.

Here is the working demo. The left side holds a synthetic Japanese dispensing record. The center shows a named policy. The patient generates a Groth16 proof in the browser, using an issuer-signed record as a private witness. The verifier checks that signature, the treatment condition, the date window, and a holder binding. The result is a receipt with only the facts that the relying party needs.

Now change the scenario. With an ineligible treatment, the circuit cannot produce a valid proof. The verifier rejects it. This is a real cryptographic boundary in the prototype, not a UI flag.

We also connect the proof to the wider Ethereum stack. ENS gives policies names that humans can inspect. The Solidity gate expresses how a named eligibility proof could be accepted onchain. World IDKit adds a separate proof that the claimant is a unique human, so the same benefit cannot be redeemed again under another account. Identity and medical details stay separate.

For Japanese health data, provenance matters. We built an adapter for the MyNaPortal medication export format and a second circuit that proves a medication code belongs to an allowed set while hiding the code behind a SHA-256 commitment. A TLS notarization experiment validates the transport layer. The next integration step is binding an actual authenticated health portal response to that medical commitment.

MynaHealth points toward a different default: a medical record can stay with its holder while institutions verify only the condition they are authorized to ask about. Prove the condition. Keep the record.
